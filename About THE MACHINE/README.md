THE MACHINE Version History
===
*These have all been written in retrospective after an idea from our fellow F.E. team from the Benjamin Harrison [The Primis](https://github.com/SebicheParrilla/The-primis)

Ye First ancestor:

<img height="500" src="Ye ancestors pics/Ye Very First Ancestor.JPG">

This is the very first prototype that even resembles teh current state of THE MACHINE. Built around August or September of 2025, this was before the team was made, before categories were decided, even before we knew which categories existed. The soul purpose of this version was went to be an rc car, as you can see we have just two motor, the Lego Spike Medium Motor and the Lego Boost Motor, with regular Lego Spike software, this never worked, but as of right now with Pybricks, who know what we could do with the Lego Boost Motor.

Ye Second Ancestor:

<img height="500" src="Ye ancestors pics/Ye closest ancestor.JPG">

This iteration is more similar to V. Juan, you could even say that this is V. Juan in his early stages as he posseses the same rims and components but they're placed differently and due to side Distance Sensor placement he cannot steer at all as they're touching the sensor and the front wheel mount aren't the dedicated Lego piece, instead being a subtitute built out of Lego.

[V. Chew](V.%20Chew/README.md):

<img height="250" width="700" src="V. Chew/V. Chew pictures/IMG_E1810.JPG">

Shorter chasis, same components (for but 1 competition) and a new programming language which you can actually look at in Github in the form of [Micropython](https://docs.micropython.org/en/latest/), or more specifically [Pybricks](https://pybricks.com/), a modified version of Micropython meant for Lego microcontrollers like the Lego Spike Prime Hub. But, as hinted to earlier, he didn't stay like this for long:

<img src="V. Chew/V. Chew pictures/IMG_1626.JPG">

V. Chew R(udolf) is our first version of V. Chew that incorporates non Lego electronics with the use of a [Cytron Maker Pi RP2040](https://www.cytron.io/p-maker-pi-rp2040-simplifying-robotics-with-raspberry-pi-rp2040) located under the Lego Spike Hub, and a [APDS-9960](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/apds-9960-rgb-and-gesture-sensor-hookup-guide/all) in replacement of the official Lego Color Sensor, but it didn't stay in use for long as in testing it showed to be quite in accurate at distances upward of about 2cm aprox so we did an upgrade:

<img height=175 width=700 src="V. Chew/V. Chew pictures/IMG_1698.jpg">

As you might have noticed, V. Chew has a thing mounted on him, he can see you clearly now cause we've decided to mount a Huskylens 1 to V. Chew for obstacle detection.
